## C. Open-question policy / instructions to developer

Each `OQ-XX` names a specific unresolved business rule, not just “TBD.” The row indicates what exists now, what must **not** be invented, and whether implementation of that aspect should wait.

- **BLOCKER:** Do not implement a final business rule for the affected function until answered; continue unrelated confirmed foundational work.
- **NON-BLOCKING:** Implement the approved minimum behavior and preserve an extension point. Don't silently add unapproved behavior.
- **DECISION-LATER:** Pending detailed workflow or content decisions. A safe minimal interface may be built if already confirmed, but no inferred default beyond that.

## D. Priorities for the next discussions

**Owner direction, 2026-10-19:** Stop turning every small accounting/implementation implication into another standalone approval question. First audit the original **core post-sale functionality** against this specification and identify genuinely consequential missing capabilities, unclear sources of truth, cross-module business rules and implementation blockers. Ask only questions that materially affect operation, architecture, financial correctness or scope. Group related issues; use established decisions to resolve obvious derivations without re-asking the owner. Record lower-priority edge cases as explicitly OPEN with safe developer boundaries instead of forcing sequential micro-decisions.

High-impact areas still to audit first (not automatically all separate numbered decisions):

1. **Contract-to-operation handoff and essential data completeness:** per-tour adult/child counts and dated-stay hotel default/override are now **confirmed** (`CON-012`, `RES-020`); focus next on free-text tour/provider exceptions and data that could leave services silently unbookable (`OQ-04`, `OQ-07`, `OQ-12`).
2. **Financial reporting that is actually reliable:** contract total vs internal sold-tour price allocations, currency/FX, provider cost attribution and period/metric definitions (`OQ-22`–`OQ-24`).
3. **Services across multiple providers, transfers and tariff variations:** required operational linkage and necessary editable fallbacks (`OQ-09`, `OQ-10`, `OQ-14`).
4. **Operational readiness safeguards:** arrival/service urgency and work queue, plus safe supplier-link access and minimum permissions for launch (`OQ-26`, `OQ-28`, `OQ-29`, `OQ-35`).

Secondary details (refund corrections, uncommon transitions, electronic signatures, optional wallet extensions, exact UX) stay documented for later unless they emerge as true blockers. The goal is a reliable developer handoff with minimal owner rework—not a high decision count.

## E. Change-control protocol

When a new owner decision is confirmed:

1. Allocate the next `DEC-###`; write exact decision, motivating example, implementation now, exclusions and unresolved follow-ups.
2. Resolve/update affected `OQ-##` and relevant `CON-*`, `RES-*`, `PAY-*`, etc. requirements. Never silently erase old context or conflate approval with a proposed recommendation.
3. Update `03_Data_Architecture_and_Rules.md` if identity, lifecycle, propagation or source of truth changes.
4. Add or modify `05_Acceptance_Scenarios.md` cases for changed behavior.
5. Validate cross-file references and revise `README.md` version/change note. Keep Git history or prior ZIPs intact.
6. Run a full package consistency check after 3–5 decisions, after any cross-module change, and immediately before developer handoff.

**Next numbered decision, if a genuinely material unresolved question warrants one: `DEC-022`.** The v0.1.36 supplier rules are confirmed cross-module clarifications, not new sequential numbered decisions.
